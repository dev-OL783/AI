Artefact 9: Purpose – Hacking an AI Chatbot
For this week’s portfolio artefact, you will attempt to bypass the guardrails of a topic-restricted AI chatbot using an adversarial or edge-case prompt. We provide a very basic implementation of a CLI chatbot that is only allowed to chat about gardening-related topics. Your task is to find a prompt (or a short sequence of prompts) that causes it to respond on a topic other than gardening, and to reflect on why it works.

The Chatbot
This script implements a simple gardening assistant: gardening_demo.pyDownload gardening_demo.py. Its only (weak) restriction is described in the system prompt, and it is guarded by the default Azure guardrails against adversarial attacks. Run it using your IFB220 API key (see below for setup instructions) and interact with it to understand its behaviour before attempting to bypass it.

Setting up an environment variable. The script reads your key from an environment variable called API_KEY. Set it before running the script:

macOS / Linux: export API_KEY=your_key_here
Windows (PowerShell): $env:API_KEY = "your_key_here"
Your key is available from the IFB220 Developer PortalLinks to an external site..
Detailed instructions:
Your task is to craft an adversarial or edge-case prompt that causes the gardening chatbot to respond substantively on a topic that is clearly unrelated to gardening. A successful submission must satisfy all of the following:

The chatbot’s response is clearly and substantively off-topic (not just a brief acknowledgement before returning to gardening).
Your prompt uses a technique covered in the lecture or discussed during the Week 9 workshop activities (e.g. roleplay framing, fictional scenario, logical flip, gradual escalation, context stuffing, or focus shifting).
Your submission includes the exact prompt text (or sequence of prompts) that produced the off-topic response, along with a brief explanation of the technique used and why you believe it worked.
You may use AI tools to help you develop, refine, or analyse your adversarial prompt. If you do, describe how in your reflection.
